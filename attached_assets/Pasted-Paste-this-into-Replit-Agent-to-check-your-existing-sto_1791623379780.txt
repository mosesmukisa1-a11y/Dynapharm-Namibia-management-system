Debug and verify this stock management application from end to end. Inspect the existing code, database, and user interface before making changes. Work with the application’s current technology stack.

The required workflow is:
Import → Warehouse receipt → Branch dispatch → Stock in transit → Branch receipt → Sales → Returns and adjustments → Stock and sales reports.

Do not delete or overwrite existing business data. Use a separate test database or isolated test environment. Do not connect tests to live payments or other live external services.

1. CHECK THE CURRENT SETUP

Identify:
- The application framework and database.
- How products, suppliers, warehouses, branches, batches, barcodes, and expiry dates are stored.
- How purchases, receipts, transfers, sales, returns, and adjustments are recorded.
- How current stock balances and sales reports are calculated.
- Missing features, broken connections, and incorrect calculations.

2. DEBUG STOCK MOVEMENTS

Verify and fix these rules:
- Purchase orders do not increase physical stock before receipt.
- Receiving an import increases stock at the receiving warehouse once.
- Dispatch reduces warehouse stock and increases stock in transit.
- Branch receipt reduces stock in transit and increases branch stock.
- Internal transfers do not change total company stock.
- Partial receipts, damaged deliveries, and shortages are recorded explicitly.
- A completed sale deducts the correct quantity from the correct branch and batch.
- Saleable customer returns increase available stock once.
- Damaged or expired returns go into unavailable stock.
- Supplier returns remove stock from the correct location.
- Damage, expiry, loss, and other adjustments require reasons and an audit trail.
- Repeated requests, double clicks, or retries cannot create duplicate movements.
- Concurrent sales cannot sell the same remaining stock twice.
- Related transaction updates either succeed together or roll back together.
- Corrections preserve transaction history through reversals or traceable adjustments.

Distinguish physical stock, available stock, reserved stock, unavailable stock, and stock in transit. Prevent negative available stock unless an explicit business rule allows it.

3. CHECK PRODUCTS, BARCODES, AND EXPIRY

Verify:
- Barcodes identify the correct product and pack size.
- Barcode values retain leading zeros.
- Duplicate barcode assignments are prevented.
- Carton, box, and individual-item quantities convert correctly.
- Batch numbers and expiry dates follow stock through every movement.
- Earliest-expiring eligible stock is selected first.
- Expired stock cannot be sold.
- Expiry and low-stock alerts use configurable thresholds.

4. CHECK CALCULATIONS AND REPORTS

For each location, verify:
Closing physical stock =
Opening physical stock
+ Receipts
+ Transfers in
+ Customer returns received
− Sales
− Transfers out
− Supplier returns
− Write-offs
± Approved adjustments.

Moving stock from available to damaged or expired status must not remove it from physical stock until it is written off or otherwise leaves the business.

Verify reports for:
- Current stock by product, location, batch, and stock status.
- Stock in transit.
- Stock movement history.
- Sales quantities, refunds, and net sales.
- Stock valuation and cost of goods sold using the configured costing method.
- Gross profit using consistent costs and revenue.
- Low stock, slow-moving stock, and approaching expiry.

Use the same underlying stock records for dashboards and reports. Check date filters and the configured business timezone.
